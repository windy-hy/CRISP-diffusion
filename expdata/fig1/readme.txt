\textbf{Overview and training framework of DiffCRISP.}
\textbf{a,} Overview of the DiffCRISP framework for single-cell perturbation prediction. DiffCRISP integrates single-cell context, drug chemical information, and dose to model conditional drug-induced transcriptional responses. Control-cell context is represented using precomputed single-cell foundation-model (scFM) embeddings, whereas drug information is encoded from molecular structure together with a log-transformed dose. During training, observed treated gene-expression profiles provide the target perturbation state. The trained model generates predicted treated-cell populations that can subsequently be evaluated in unseen-cell-type prediction, unseen-drug zero-shot prediction, cross-platform transfer, and single-cell distribution analyses.
\textbf{b,} Two-stage training and inference procedure of DiffCRISP. In Stage I, a gene-expression autoencoder is pretrained to map single-cell expression profiles into a compact latent representation and reconstruct the original expression profiles. In Stage II, the pretrained autoencoder is frozen and a conditional latent diffusion model is trained. The paired-control branch projects scFM embeddings into a control-cell latent representation, while the drug branch projects the molecular embedding and dose into a drug latent representation. These representations are combined to form the semantic condition for the diffusion model. The latent representation of the observed treated cells serves as the diffusion target, and the conditional denoiser is optimized to predict the noise added at randomly sampled diffusion timesteps. During inference, paired control-cell embeddings together with the target drug and dose are used to condition DDIM sampling, and the generated latent states are decoded by the frozen gene decoder to obtain predicted perturbed gene-expression profiles.
