\caption{
\textbf{Overview and training framework of DiffCRISP.}
\textbf{a,} Overview of the DiffCRISP framework for single-cell perturbation prediction. DiffCRISP integrates single-cell context, drug chemical information, and dose to model conditional drug-induced transcriptional responses. Control-cell context is represented using precomputed single-cell foundation-model (scFM) embeddings, whereas drug information is encoded from molecular structure together with a log-transformed dose. During training, observed treated gene-expression profiles provide the target perturbation state. The trained model generates predicted treated-cell populations that can subsequently be evaluated in unseen-cell-type prediction, unseen-drug zero-shot prediction, cross-platform transfer, and single-cell distribution analyses.
\textbf{b,} Two-stage training and inference procedure of DiffCRISP. In Stage I, a gene-expression autoencoder is pretrained to map single-cell expression profiles into a compact latent representation and reconstruct the original expression profiles. In Stage II, the pretrained autoencoder is frozen and a conditional latent diffusion model is trained. The paired-control branch projects scFM embeddings into a control-cell latent representation, while the drug branch projects the molecular embedding and dose into a drug latent representation. These representations are combined to form the semantic condition for the diffusion model. The latent representation of the observed treated cells serves as the diffusion target, and the conditional denoiser is optimized to predict the noise added at randomly sampled diffusion timesteps. During inference, paired control-cell embeddings together with the target drug and dose are used to condition DDIM sampling, and the generated latent states are decoded by the frozen gene decoder to obtain predicted perturbed gene-expression profiles.
}

\textbf{Distribution recovery and sampling variability of DiffCRISP.}
\textbf{a,} Comparison of population-level distribution recovery between CRISP and DiffCRISP across three data splits of the NeurIPS dataset. Bars show the mean $\pm$ SEM across perturbation conditions for Sinkhorn distance, centered Sinkhorn distance, and variance log-MAE, with lower values indicating better recovery.

\textbf{c--d,} Sampling variability across multiple K562 drug perturbations in the SciPlex3 dataset.
\textbf{c,} Between-seed variation measured as the mean pairwise Sinkhorn distance among populations generated with ten independent sampling seeds for each perturbation condition. \textbf{d,} Relative sampling variation, defined as the ratio between variation among generated populations and the corresponding variation obtained by repeatedly resampling observed treated cells. Values closer to 1 indicate better agreement with the intrinsic sampling variability of the observed population. Extreme perturbation conditions outside the 2.5th--97.5th percentile range were excluded from panels \textbf{c} and \textbf{d} for visualization.
\textbf{e,} Pairwise Sinkhorn distances among populations generated with ten sampling seeds for K562 cells treated with Cerdulatinib at dose 1.0. \caption{
\textbf{Recovery of drug-responsive cellular subpopulations by DiffCRISP.}
Drug-response directions were defined for each perturbation using the top 50 genes with the largest absolute expression changes between observed treated and matched control cells. Individual cells were projected onto the corresponding response direction, and cells with response scores above the 95th percentile of the control distribution were classified as responders.
\textbf{a,} Absolute error between predicted and observed responder proportions across three data splits for CRISP and DiffCRISP. Boxes indicate the interquartile range, center lines denote medians, whiskers extend to values within 1.5$\times$ IQR, and points represent individual perturbation conditions. $P$ values were calculated using two-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests.
\textbf{b,} Perturbation-level comparison of responder-proportion errors between CRISP and DiffCRISP. The dashed diagonal indicates equal error; points below the diagonal correspond to perturbation conditions for which DiffCRISP achieves lower error.
\textbf{c,} Fraction of perturbation conditions in which DiffCRISP achieves lower responder-proportion error than CRISP for each split and overall. The dashed horizontal line indicates a 50\% win rate, and $n$ denotes the number of evaluated perturbation conditions.
\textbf{d,} Single-cell response-score distributions for Idelalisib- and Palbociclib-treated CD4$^{+}$ T cells. Curves show control, observed treated, CRISP-predicted, and DiffCRISP-predicted populations; values in parentheses indicate responder proportions, and dashed vertical lines denote the responder thresholds defined from the corresponding control populations.
}
